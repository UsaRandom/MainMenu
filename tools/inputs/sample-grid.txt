# A full card, tab by tab. What the grid looks like when there is enough on it to judge.
#
# Run with SAMPLE=1 (and SAMPLE_MIX=true for the control). Every tab here is at least three full
# rows, which is the point: a tab holding four titles tells you nothing about whether a layout is
# compact, and every screenshot this project had before this one was of a tab holding four.
#
# The interesting frames are 1 and 6. Frame 1 is Recent, the only tab where two tile shapes share
# a grid -- and on a cold card it is also where the fallback shows, because the cell is settled
# before most covers have been measured. Frame 6 is Game Boy: four columns of 140 x 140 squares,
# where the portrait tabs are five of 109 x 155. If the column count were fixed rather than per
# tab, that art would be 109 px wide and a third smaller.

wait 400            # boot plate, a 115-title scan, then a screenful of covers

fbdump              # 1: RECENT. Twelve played across six systems, so portrait and square mixed.

press r
wait 250
fbdump              # 2: FAVOURITES, also mixed

press r
wait 400            # 30 covers is six rows; the first two have to land before this means anything
fbdump              # 3: N64, five columns of portrait boxes

press down x2
wait 250
fbdump              # 4: scrolled two rows down a six-row tab

press r
wait 300
fbdump              # 5: NES, four rows

press r x2
wait 300
fbdump              # 6: GB. Square cells, four across, two rows and a peek

press r x2
wait 300
fbdump              # 7: SMS, back to portrait

exit
